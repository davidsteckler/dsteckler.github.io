// Michael Saylor · advice for young people

Upgrade the world.

Lex Fridman asked Michael Saylor what he would tell a young person entering adulthood. Saylor answered with ten short rules. They start with what you control most directly — your energy, time, mind, and body — then widen out to the people around you and what you leave behind.

// the list

Ten things worth coming back to.

Saylor gave the ten as a compact list. The notes below are short, plain-English readings of the surrounding conversation — not extra quotes. His main point is that the world contains more options than anyone can absorb, so direction matters.

01Focus your energy 02Guard your time 03Train your mind 04Train your body 05Think for yourself 06Curate your friends 07Curate your environment 08Keep your promises 09Stay cheerful + constructive 10Upgrade the world
01 / 10

Focus your energy.

Choose a small number of things that deserve your best effort. Saylor's point is that trying to pursue everything at once makes it hard to become exceptional at anything.

Choose what gets your best
02 / 10

Guard your time.

There is an endless supply of things willing to absorb your attention. Treat your time like something valuable enough to protect deliberately.

Protect the scarce resource
03 / 10

Train your mind.

Keep learning. Improve how you reason, how you judge information, and how you make decisions. A capable mind is built through repeated use.

Sharpen the instrument
04 / 10

Train your body.

Your ability to work, think, endure, and recover is tied to the body carrying you through it. Physical training belongs in the foundation.

Build capacity
05 / 10

Think for yourself.

Do not automatically inherit the crowd's conclusions. Look at the problem, test the assumptions, and decide what you actually believe.

Own the conclusion
06 / 10

Curate your friends.

The people around you change what feels normal, ambitious, acceptable, and possible. Be intentional about who gets close enough to influence you.

Choose your circle
07 / 10

Curate your environment.

What surrounds you keeps nudging your behavior. Shape your physical and digital environment so the things you value are easier to keep doing.

Design the defaults
08 / 10

Keep your promises.

Reliability compounds. Make commitments carefully, then become the kind of person whose word carries weight because people have seen you honor it.

Make your word mean something
09 / 10

Stay cheerful and constructive.

Bring useful energy to problems. Look for what can be improved, built, repaired, or learned instead of spending all your effort feeding the problem.

Add useful energy
10 / 10

Upgrade the world.

The list ends by turning outward. Build something, improve something, teach something, solve something. Leave some part of the world better than you found it.

Leave an improvement behind
10

Pick one for today.

Ten rules are easy to admire and easy to forget. Pick one and give it your attention for the day.
