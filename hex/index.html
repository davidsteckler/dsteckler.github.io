Why does a solid-colored word have so many colors?

The eyedropper reads one displayed pixel. A letter’s edge can have a different color from its center.

Letters cut across a square grid. When an edge covers part of a pixel, the computer blends the letter’s color with the background. This smoothing is called antialiasing.

Some screens use another trick. Many LCD pixels contain red, green, and blue parts. Text rendering such as Windows ClearType can control those parts separately. Zooming in can reveal colored fringes, even around black letters.

The colors you see depend on the browser, operating system, display, and scaling. The magnifier shows the rendered pixels; it doesn’t show the screen’s physical parts.
