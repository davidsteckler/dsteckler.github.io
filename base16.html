Hexadecimal · Base 16

Why the powers start at zero

The rightmost place is 160 = 1. Moving one place left multiplies its value by 16. The next places are 161, 162, and 163. Change a digit above to see how much it contributes.

The extra digits mean A = 10, B = 11, C = 12, D = 13, E = 14, and F = 15. Four binary bits have 2⁴ = 16 patterns, so one hexadecimal digit can stand for four bits.

In a hex color such as #FF8040, the six digits form three bytes: FF for red, 80 for green, and 40 for blue. FF = 255, 80 = 128, and 40 = 64 in decimal.
